IMPORTANT IRRIGATION RECOMMENDATIONS:

Evapotranspiration (ET) & Moisture-Based Scheduling

  • Calculate Net Water Requirement: Match irrigation volume to crop water loss (ETc). (Apply only the difference between plant water use and effective rainfall.)
  • Maintain the 50% Depletion Limit: Do not allow Management Allowed Depletion (MAD) to exceed 50% of available water capacity in the root zone before applying the next irrigation cycle.
  • Monitor Soil Tension:

  1. Coarse/Sandy Soil: Irrigate when soil water tension reaches 20–30 kPa.
  2. Medium/Loam Soil: Irrigate when soil water tension reaches 40–50 kPa.
  3. Heavy/Clay Soil: Irrigate when soil water tension reaches 50–70 kPa.

WATER CONSERVATION PRACTICES

Smart irrigation methods save water by delivering it precisely when and where crops need it most. Drip irrigation systems apply water directly to plant roots drop by drop, which drastically reduces evaporation and wind drift. Implementing high-efficiency Drip Irrigation (DI) systems allows an individual emitter to release just 2,000 cm3 to 4,000 cm3 of water per hour directly to the plant base. This targeted micro-application cuts farm water waste by 30% to 50% compared to traditional flood methods. Farmers combine this with data-driven irrigation scheduling and soil moisture sensors to ensure fields are watered only when actual soil deficits exist, preventing overwatering.Soil and land management practices focus on trapping moisture directly within the earth. Applying organic mulch, such as straw or wood chips, shields the topsoil from solar radiation and slows down surface evaporation. Additionally, practicing no-till farming and planting cover crops builds organic matter, improves soil structure, and significantly increases the land's natural water-holding capacity.
